# Myelin — Contributors, Collaboration & Reciprocity Notes

*Continuation of `myelin-distribution-design-and-open-source-culture.md`, capturing the discussion that followed it.*

---

## 1. Contributor Recognition Should Be More Than a Credits Page

A dedicated contributor page was proposed, but the idea quickly expanded beyond a conventional acknowledgements or credits section.

The stronger concept is:

> **A public record of creative participation where contributed work can retain value even when it is not used directly in the product.**

This matters because Myelin is intended to welcome contributions from people across disciplines, not only programmers.

Possible contributors include:

- software engineers;
- designers;
- photographers;
- illustrators;
- typographers;
- researchers;
- teachers;
- editors;
- accessibility specialists;
- data/visualization practitioners;
- other creators whose work can improve the product or the publishing ecosystem.

A contribution should not disappear simply because it does not become part of Myelin's core UI or codebase.

Someone may produce:

- a photographic series;
- an illustration set;
- a design exploration;
- a typography study;
- an interaction concept;
- a dataset;
- a teaching resource;
- a visual explanation;
- an editorial experiment;

and that work may still be valuable elsewhere in the Myelin ecosystem.

---

## 2. Contributor Profiles

Contributor profiles were identified as an important part of this system.

A profile could preserve:

- the contributor's identity;
- their discipline;
- their Myelin contributions;
- where those contributions were used;
- collaborations they participated in;
- publications containing their work;
- links to their own portfolio/site;
- whether they are open to further collaboration.

Over time, a contributor profile could become a durable record such as:

> Designed an early annotation interaction  
> Photography used in an indexed field report  
> Created interactive figures for three publications  
> Contributed accessibility improvements  
> Developed a visual language for datasets

The important principle is that attribution should remain visible and durable.

Even if a contribution is later replaced or not used directly, the contribution itself can still remain part of Myelin's creative history.

---

## 3. Good Work Should Be Able to Find a Second Life

One of the most useful ideas was that unused work should not automatically become wasted work.

A contributor's work could potentially be discovered and integrated into another author's publication.

Examples:

**researcher + photographer**  
→ field report or visual investigation

**historian + illustrator**  
→ annotated visual essay

**scientist + interaction designer**  
→ explorable experiment

**teacher + typographer**  
→ unusually strong lesson material

**engineer + information designer**  
→ comprehensible systems investigation

This turns Myelin into more than a place where isolated people publish.

It becomes a place where different forms of expertise can combine.

The standard should therefore not be:

> “Everything contributed must ship.”

It should be:

> **Good work should be acknowledged, discoverable, and given opportunities to connect with other good work.**

---

## 4. Collaboration Discovery

The contributor/creator system could include lightweight collaboration signals, but one boundary should be explicit in the product copy from the beginning:

> **This is discovery of existing work, not a request board.**

Authors can discover work that someone has already chosen to publish and ask whether they may reuse, reference, adapt, or build on it. Creators are never expected to create something new for another person simply because they are visible on Myelin.

Suggested product copy:

> **Authors can ask to reuse or build on your published work. You're never asked to create something new for someone else.**

A person might mark existing work or a profile with statuses such as:

- open to collaboration;
- open to reuse/adaptation;
- open to inclusion in publications;
- open to discussion about existing work;
- interested in Myelin product work.

The goal is not to create a generic social network, freelance marketplace, commission board, or system where creators receive unsolicited production requests.

It is to make serious cross-disciplinary collaboration easier when already-existing work has a clear reason to connect with another author's project.

A possible flow:

**creator publishes existing work  
→ work remains attached to their profile  
→ creator optionally marks it as open to reuse/collaboration  
→ another author discovers it  
→ they ask whether they may incorporate or build on that existing work  
→ the creator decides whether to participate  
→ the resulting publication credits both people clearly**

---


### Discovery of Existing Work, Not a Request Board

This distinction should be explicit in the product itself.

Myelin collaboration is intended to help people discover work that already exists and ask whether it can be reused, adapted, cited, extended, or incorporated into another publication.

It is **not** a request board where creators are expected to make bespoke work for strangers.

Suggested product copy:

> **Authors can ask to reuse or build on your published work; you're never asked to create something new for someone else.**

That sentence captures the intended boundary clearly.

A photographer can publish an existing series and mark it as open to collaboration. An author may later ask whether selected images can be incorporated into a publication.

An illustrator can publish diagrams or a visual study and allow others to ask about adapting or building on them.

A researcher can publish a dataset, experiment, or figure and allow others to request reuse or extension.

The creator is always responding to interest in work they have already chosen to publish. They are not being placed into an unpaid commission queue.


## 5. Important Terminology Distinction

A useful distinction emerged between three overlapping identities.

### Authors

People who publish substantive work on Myelin.

### Creators / Collaborators

People who publish or contribute photography, illustration, design, datasets, visual material, or other work that may be used in collaboration with authors.

### Contributors

People who directly help build or improve Myelin itself through code, design, documentation, accessibility, research, editorial work, visual systems, templates, teaching resources, and similar contributions.

One person can be all three.

This distinction prevents the word **contributor** from becoming overly broad and avoids implying that every person publishing creative work is working *for Myelin*.

## 6. The Free-Labour Concern

A significant concern was raised:

> Would people want to contribute if the founder is the only person directly making money from the product?

This is a real concern if the model is framed poorly.

The problematic framing would be:

> “Make things for Myelin for free and maybe you will get exposure.”

That would feel exploitative, particularly for photographers, illustrators, designers, editors, and other creative professionals.

However, that is **not** the intended model.

The intended model is closer to platforms such as Behance or Dribbble:

- people post work because the work is theirs;
- they retain visible authorship;
- the platform gives the work another place to exist;
- other people can discover it;
- collaboration can emerge from it;
- the work can be incorporated into larger projects with explicit credit.

The key difference is that Myelin could offer more than static portfolio exposure.

A photographer's work could become part of an indexed essay.

An illustrator's work could become part of an interactive explanation.

A designer's visual system could become part of a research publication.

A dataset or diagram could be incorporated into another author's argument.

Myelin therefore has the potential to turn published work into **active intellectual material**, not merely a static portfolio item.

---

## 7. Do Not Promise “Exposure”

The phrase “more eyes on your work” was considered risky as a primary pitch.

Early Myelin cannot guarantee audience size, and promising exposure would create the wrong incentive structure.

The platform should instead offer benefits it can actually control:

- permanent attribution;
- excellent presentation;
- a creator/contributor profile;
- links back to the creator's own site;
- rich publication tools;
- collaboration discovery;
- the ability for work to become part of serious publications;
- clear credit when incorporated into another person's work;
- access to whatever audience Myelin genuinely develops over time.

If Myelin later develops substantial authority and reach, that becomes additional upside rather than a promise used to obtain early contributions.

---

## 8. Attribution Inside Publications

When a publication uses work from multiple people, attribution should be first-class rather than hidden at the bottom of the page.

For example:

> **Written by A**  
> **Photography by B**  
> **Interactive figures by C**

Each name could link to the person's Myelin profile.

This reinforces the idea that different kinds of expertise are equally legitimate parts of a serious publication.

It also gives collaborators a concrete benefit: their work remains visibly associated with the final result.

---

## 9. Myelin as a Cross-Disciplinary Discovery Layer

A useful distinction emerged when comparing this idea with conventional portfolio platforms.

Behance or Dribbble primarily answer:

> “What has this person made?”

Myelin could additionally answer:

> **“What could this person's work become when combined with somebody else's expertise?”**

That is potentially a much more interesting network effect.

The aim is not to become a generic creator marketplace.

The aim is to create enough structure that high-quality work from different disciplines can find useful connections.

---

## 10. Complimentary Publisher Subscription for Contributors

A concrete incentive was proposed:

> **Meaningful Myelin contributors receive the $5 publishing/sync subscription for free.**

This is attractive because the marginal cost of the lower-tier subscription is expected to be very low.

It also creates immediate, concrete reciprocity.

Instead of offering only prestige or future exposure, Myelin gives contributors an actual product benefit they would otherwise pay for.

A contributor receives:

- recognition for helping Myelin;
- a contributor profile;
- publishing access;
- sync access;
- the ability to maintain their own public work on Myelin;
- access to collaboration opportunities;
- a reason to remain active in the ecosystem.

Myelin receives:

- more contributors who are also active users;
- more high-quality public work;
- better product feedback from people who help shape the platform;
- more natural demonstrations of the publishing system;
- a stronger connection between the open-source project and the public publishing community.

---

## 11. Contribution → Publishing Loop

The complimentary subscription creates a useful positive loop:

**contribute to Myelin  
→ receive Publisher access  
→ publish your own work  
→ attract readers or collaborators  
→ understand Myelin more deeply as a user  
→ potentially contribute again**

This is much healthier than:

**contribute work  
→ receive vague exposure**

The incentive is direct, useful, and aligned with the product.

---

## 12. Defining “Contributor”

The complimentary subscription should not require an opaque point system, but some basic definition is necessary.

A single typo fix should probably not automatically create a lifetime entitlement, while a meaningful contribution should be recognized generously.

A practical early rule could be:

> **Meaningful accepted contributions that materially improve Myelin qualify for contributor benefits.**

This can include:

- code;
- product or interaction design;
- documentation;
- accessibility;
- research;
- illustration;
- photography;
- editorial work;
- teaching resources;
- templates;
- other substantial contributions.

Initially this can remain discretionary rather than bureaucratic.

---

## 13. Permanent Recognition vs Ongoing Benefits

A useful distinction is:

### Attribution and Contributor History

Permanent.

If someone meaningfully contributed to Myelin, that record should not disappear merely because they later become inactive.

### Complimentary Subscription

Potentially linked to active contribution or granted permanently for major contributions.

A possible model:

- meaningful active contributors receive Publisher access at no cost;
- major or foundational contributors may receive lifetime access;
- contributor profiles and historical attribution remain permanently visible.

This allows generosity without creating an unbounded entitlement system.

---

## 14. Why This Helps Myelin's Cold Start

Contributors are also likely to be unusually strong early publishers.

Giving them free publishing access helps solve two problems at once:

1. rewarding contribution;
2. filling the public platform with interesting work.

The people helping shape Myelin may become some of the first people demonstrating what Myelin can actually publish.

This makes the public ecosystem feel less artificial because the early content comes from people already invested in the project.

## 15. Ethical Boundary

The core ethical distinction should remain explicit:


A core product-level rule should make the boundary unambiguous:

> **Myelin is for discovery of existing published work, not requests for unpaid new work.**

Authors may ask to reuse, cite, adapt, or build on something a creator has already published. Creators should never be implicitly expected to produce something new merely because another user wants it.



> **People should not be expected to produce bespoke unpaid work for Myelin or for other Myelin authors in exchange for hypothetical future prestige.**

Myelin's collaboration model should begin with discovery of work that already exists.

The clearest product rule is:

> **Authors can ask to reuse or build on your published work. You're never asked to create something new for someone else.**

Instead:

- people publish their own work voluntarily;
- creators retain clear authorship;
- discovery begins from existing published work;
- reuse or adaptation requires the creator's permission;
- creators are free to decline without consequence;
- collaboration is optional;
- attribution is durable;
- Myelin provides concrete tools and benefits;
- contributor status is earned through genuine participation;
- any future platform authority is additional upside, not promised compensation.

That protects both the contributor relationship and Myelin's culture, while preventing the collaboration layer from drifting into unpaid commissions or a request marketplace.

---

## 16. Distilled Direction

The contributor system is evolving into something larger than a GitHub-style acknowledgements page.

The strongest version is:

> **A public network of people who help build Myelin, publish their own work, discover one another, and combine different forms of expertise into stronger publications.**

The contributor profile preserves the history of who helped create the medium.

The creator/author profiles preserve the work itself.

The collaboration layer lets work move between people rather than becoming static.

And complimentary Publisher access creates a concrete form of reciprocity that costs Myelin little while giving contributors a real reason to stay involved and publish.

This supports the broader cultural ambition:

> **Myelin should not only be open source in code. It should be an open creative and intellectual system where excellent work from many disciplines can meaningfully participate.**
