Abstract

Tool-call accuracy is usually treated as a property of a model. We find that, below roughly 14B parameters, it is substantially a property of the harness the model runs inside. Across a controlled grid of five model sizes and two harness conditions, an 8B model in an instrumented harness outperforms a 20B model in a naive one. We isolate the mechanism to tool-schema typing, quantify the cost of the stronger harness, and report two independent reproductions — one of which fails to reproduce the result, for a reason we believe we have identified but have not confirmed.

Introduction

There is a strong intuition in applied work that a bigger model is a better agent. It is probably right. It is also, in the range where most tool-using systems actually run, not the dominant variable.

We built a 240-task suite of multi-turn tool calls, stratified by turn count and tool counttasksuite-v4, and ran five model sizes from 8B to 70B through two harness conditions. The instrumented harness constrains tool arguments to a typed schemadraft-2026-09, re-asks once on a validation failure, and keeps an explicit turn state. The naive harness exposes the same tools as free strings and relies on the model to stay in format.

The headline is a crossover. At 8B, the instrumented harness turns a 41.2% model into an 88.1% model. At 20B, the same harness moves 74.3% to 90.8%. The 8B instrumented model therefore beats the 20B naive model by fourteen pointsTable 4.2 — and by about eight once our own quantisation error in that table is correctederratum.

This is not a new observation in direction. Vendors have been saying some version of it for two yearsCDNW slides, and an industry survey reports the same ordering of failure modesagentweekly without isolating the cause. What is new here is the controlled manipulation, the isolation of a mechanism, and the willingness to publish a reproduction that does not reproducereka's log.

Related work

Tanaka et al. report a crossover below 10B parameters across four model families, with scaffolding quality predicting completion better than parameter countTable 3. Their rig is independent of ours — a different harness, a different task suite, a different evaluation stack — and their crossover sits in the same place. That convergence is the strongest evidence in this paper, and it is evidence we did not produce.

Svensson argues the whole framing is confounded: that scaffold interventions and task difficulty are not independent, and that "harness" bundles at least four variables that published comparisons cannot separate§4. We think this objection is largely correct and we have tried to answer it by varying one component at a time rather than swapping a whole harness. It is not fully answered.

Method

Task suite

240 tasks across eight categories, stratified by turn count (2 to 5) and tool count (1 to 3). Exact-match scoring on the full emitted call, not on tool selection alone — a call with the right tool and a malformed argument scores zero. Three seeds per cell. The suite itself is attachedtasksuite-v4.

Models and serving

Five sizes from 8B to 70B, all Qwen2.5 instruct, served through llama.cpp with matched quantisation. This was not true of the original run and is the subject of an open erratum; the original 20B baseline was q4_k_m and the 8B was q4_0.

Harness conditions

Both conditions expose identical tool descriptions. They differ in three ways: argument typing (enum vs. free string), retry policy (one re-ask vs. none), and turn state (explicit vs. implicit). We vary them separately rather than swapping the whole harness.

Results

The naive condition rises from 41.2% at 8B to 73.8% at 14B, and then flattens — 74.3% at 20B, 81.6% at 32B, 83.9% at 70B. The instrumented condition rises from 88.1% to 96.0% across the same range and flattens for the same reason: past a certain point the remaining failures are no longer format failures.

The gap is 46.9 points at 8B and 12.1 at 70B. The two curves cross at 14B. The complete grid is attached as a figureFigure 2 and the per-task breakdown as a datasettasksuite-v4.

We have included the 32B and 70B cells specifically because they are where the claim stops mattering. If you are running a 32B model, this paper is not for you, and we would rather say so here than let you infer it from a headline number.

Where the effect comes from

Svensson's objection is that "harness" bundles several variables. We agree, so we varied them separately. Turning the harness from naive to instrumented in a single step is uninformative. Turning on argument typing alone, with retry policy and turn state fixed, recovers most of the effectexp-d: string-typed tool surfaces complete 38.1% of three-turn chains, and enum-typed surfaces complete 81.7%.

The dominant naive-harness failure is not choosing the wrong tool. It is choosing the right tool and emitting an argument the runtime cannot dispatch — and then failing to recover on the next turn, because there is no turn state to recover with. This is why the naive column collapses fastest exactly where turn count risesper-task breakdown. A two-turn task shows a modest gap; a five-turn task shows almost none.

We should be clear about what this does not establish. It establishes that typing is a large component of the observed harness effect on this suite. It does not establish that typing is the whole of it.

What it costs

A harness is not free, and any honest version of this claim has to say what it buysexp-e. Accuracy on the 8B rises 41 points; spend rises about 165%, from $4.11 to $11.72 per thousand tasks. The 20B model gets both more accurate and more expensive — the stronger harness narrows its cost disadvantage by only about a third. Wall clock roughly doubles at 8B, so if your binding constraint is latency this is a different trade entirely.

So the honest version of the headline is not "use a smaller model." It is: below 14B, spend on the harness before you spend on parameters, and know that you are buying it with tokens.

Threats to validity

Our own quantisation error. The published Table 4.2 compared a q4_0 8B against a q4_k_m 20Berratum. A matched rerun moves the 20B naive cell to about 80.1%, cutting the headline margin from fourteen points to roughly eight. The direction survives. The number in the abstract was wrong until this draft.

We previously overclaimed. The first version of this paper said harness quality was the main lever, generally. A three-turn chain benchmark on 9 August showed the effect is concentrated in long chains and near-zero for single-turn tasks. That draft was superseded rather than deletedv1.

Confounding with task suite. Svensson's central point§4 stands: a scaffold that lowers the difficulty of a task will look like a capability improvement. Our defence is that typing is varied against a fixed task suite and fixed tool descriptions, but we have not run the orthogonal suite this objection calls for.

One suite, one model family. All five sizes are Qwen2.5 instruct. Tanaka's four families are a real check on the crossover's locationTable 3, but the effect sizes here are single-family.

No claim about reasoning. The failures we measure are format and recovery failures. We find no evidence that a stronger harness improves reasoning, and the 70B cell suggests it does not.

Reproductions

Two independent attempts, opposite outcomes.

Reka re-ran the grid on an AMD 7900X3D with the same harness version and a matched task file, and found the 8B behind by about 3.7 points rather than aheadher log. She flagged that our two baseline cells appeared to use different quantisations. That is correct and it is our error.

Nakamura re-ran on an RTX 4090 with matched quantisation on both sides and reproduced the crossover closely, with the 8B instrumented model within 2.7 points of the 20B naive modelhis log. The quantisation explanation is consistent with this pair of outcomes but does not prove it — a hardware-specific explanation would fit equally well.

Where the mismatch surfaced, and where the erratum was raised, is publicthe thread. We have left the failing reproduction attached to this paper rather than withdrawing it.

Conclusion

Below about 14B parameters, tool-call accuracy is more a property of the harness than of the model. The effect is large enough to invert the usual size heuristic, isolated to argument typing, and reproduced independently by a group with no stake in it. It is also, we now know, overstated by our own error in the headline table, and contested on confounding grounds we have not fully answered.

The claim survives all three of those qualifications. That is the state of it, and the sources above are attached so a reader can check any part of that sentence rather than take it.
